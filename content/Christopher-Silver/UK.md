[Christopher Patrick Silver](Christopher-Silver) was born in Exeter, educated at King's School Bruton and Hertford College Oxford, and spent much of his career in London. His UK photographs span decades and range from intimate images of the family home in Exeter to grand architectural subjects — ruined abbeys, college buildings, cathedral towns — and chance documents of the wartime landscape. The Exeter photographs in particular carry a strong personal character; many were taken in and around the family home of his father, [Clifford Marking Silver](Clifford-Marking-Silver), the dentist.

## Exeter

CPS was born in Exeter on 21 April 1920. His father [Clifford Marking Silver](Clifford-Marking-Silver) practised as a dentist there. The large number of Exeter photographs (23 in total) suggests these were taken over many years and include extensive documentation of the family home and garden.

![The rear elevation of the Silver family home in Exeter — a substantial white-rendered Arts & Crafts house with a lean-to greenhouse, a vine-covered pergola, a garden bench, and a well-kept lawn. The greenhouse features in other photographs. This is Tresillian, the home of [Clifford Marking Silver](Clifford-Marking-Silver). It matches [David Silver](../David-Silver)'s own description and watercolour of the house.](../images/CPS-UK-Exeter-18.jpg)

![A rockery in the family garden: primulas in full bloom nestled between rough stone slabs, with irises and other plants behind. The planting is deliberate and carefully composed — a well-loved garden.](../images/CPS-UK-Exeter-01.jpg)

![Inside the family greenhouse: clay pots with small plants on a wooden staging, a crop of tomatoes ripening on the right, and a collection of cacti in the foreground. A trowel hangs on the wall. CPS's biographers note that as a house officer in Sheffield he was known for tending vegetables in the hospital garden — the horticultural interest clearly ran deep.](../images/CPS-UK-Exeter-12.jpg)

![A steam locomotive with a passenger coach passes through a station on the Exeter line — a signal box is visible on the left, and a handsome timber-framed station building on the right. Steam and speed captured with a sharp eye.](../images/CPS-UK-Exeter-06.jpg)

## Yorkshire

CPS photographed several of the great ruined abbeys and historic buildings of North Yorkshire, alongside Whitby harbour.

### Whitby

![Whitby harbour from the quayside: fishing boats and gaff-rigged sailing vessels moored along the west side, their tall masts reflecting in the still water. The old town climbs up behind.](../images/CPS-UK-Whitby-01.jpg)

![A sweeping elevated panorama over Whitby, taken from the headland near the abbey. The harbour entrance, the swing bridge, both banks of the River Esk, and the open sea beyond are all visible. One of the most technically accomplished photographs in the collection.](../images/CPS-UK-Whitby-02.jpg)

![Looking inland from the stone pier at Whitby harbour. A handsome stone capstan stands in the foreground; the quayside, bridge, and town stretch away into the distance.](../images/CPS-UK-Whitby-03.jpg)

![File:CPS UK Whitby 04.jpg](../images/CPS-UK-Whitby-04.jpg)

### Rievaulx Abbey

Rievaulx, in the North York Moors, is one of the finest ruined Cistercian abbeys in England.

![The nave and choir of Rievaulx Abbey, seen from the south. Three tiers of interlocking arches — ground-floor arcade, triforium, and clerestory — survive to near full height. A wooded valley and cottages are visible to the right. A classical CPS architectural composition.](../images/CPS-UK-Rievaulx-Abbey-01.jpg)

### Gisborough Priory

![The ruined east end of Gisborough Priory, near Guisborough in North Yorkshire — a dramatic Gothic arch rising from a lawn, framed by summer trees. The priory was founded in 1119 and largely destroyed at the Dissolution.](../images/CPS-UK-Gisborough-Priory-01.jpg)

### St William's College, York

![St William's College on College Street in York — a beautifully preserved late medieval timber-framed building dating to 1453, originally housing chantry priests of the Minster. The jettied upper storey and mullioned windows are in fine condition.](../images/CPS-UK-St-William's-College-York-01.jpg)

### Market Hall, Birmingham

![Birmingham's Market Hall, seen from across an expanse of cleared and rubble-strewn ground. ## Oxford

CPS read medicine at Hertford College, Oxford, qualifying in 1942. He photographed at least one of Oxford's great college buildings.

![The New Building at Magdalen College, Oxford — an expansive Palladian range dating to 1733, seen from across the college grounds. The arcaded ground floor and the long, symmetrical facade are imposing in the morning light.](../images/CPS-UK-Magdalen-College-Oxford-01.jpg)

## Suffolk

### St Andrew's Church

CPS photographed the interior of a church in Suffolk in considerable detail — nine plates survive. They focus on the medieval woodwork, particularly the carved bench-ends.

![Close-up of a carved wooden bench-end or pew-end in St Andrew's Church, Suffolk: a female head in profile, wearing a decorated headdress, mounted on a shaped finial. Medieval craftsmanship of the highest quality, recorded with great care.](../images/CPS-UK-St-Andrews-Church-Suffolk-01.jpg)
