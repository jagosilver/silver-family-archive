Christopher Silver photographed Cornwall extensively — its rugged coastline, harbours, and fishing villages. These images were taken on Ilford glass plate stock and show Cornwall as it looked in the early-to-mid twentieth century, before mass tourism transformed many of its communities. Cornwall was the home county of CPS's nephew Jago Silver, who still lives on the North Cornwall coast — a connection that gives these photographs a particular resonance.

## Falmouth

Falmouth, on the south Cornish coast, has one of the world's largest natural harbours. By the time CPS visited, it was already established as a sailing and boat-building centre.

![A gaff-rigged sailing boat slips through the narrow entrance to Falmouth harbour between two stone piers. Figures watch from the pier. A timeless harbour scene.](../images/CPS-Cornwall-Falmouth-01.jpg)

## Lizard Point

The Lizard is the southernmost point of mainland Britain. Its clifftops are dramatic serpentine rock, and the lighthouse has guided ships since 1619.

![The clifftops at Lizard Point, with jagged outcrops of dark serpentine rock in the foreground and the Lizard Lighthouse station visible on the headland in the distance.](../images/CPS-Cornwall-Lizard-Point-Cornwall-01.jpg)

## Mousehole

Mousehole (pronounced *Mowzel*) is a small fishing village south of Penzance, widely considered one of the most picturesque harbours in Cornwall.

![Mousehole harbour at still water: two dark fishing boats moored with perfect reflections in the calm, traditional granite cottages and stores lining the quayside, the village climbing steeply behind. One of the finest photographs in the CPS collection.](../images/CPS-Cornwall-Mousehole-Cornwall-01.jpg)

## The Gannel, Newquay

The Gannel is a tidal estuary at Newquay on the north Cornish coast — a quieter, more sheltered side of what would later become a busy seaside resort.

![Looking up the Gannel estuary from a boat called **Silver Spray** (the name is clearly visible on the bow). Two small dinghies moored mid-channel, houses visible on the valley slopes above. The boat's name is a striking coincidence — or possibly not.](../images/CPS-Cornwall-The-Gannel,-Newquay-Cornwall-01.jpg)

## Trebarwith Strand

Trebarwith Strand is a beach on the north Cornish coast near Tintagel, known for its dramatic rock formations and the small island of Gull Rock offshore.

![Trebarwith Strand at mid-tide: surf washing over dark rocks scattered across the sand, with a flat-topped cliff to the right. The broad sky and rough sea give the scene a wild, wintry quality.](../images/CPS-Cornwall-Trebarwith-Strand-Cornwall-01.jpg)

![Looking out to sea from above Trebarwith Strand — Gull Rock, the distinctive sea stack, sits in the centre of the bay, with the headland of Penhalvean on the left. Calm conditions and a light-filled sea.](../images/CPS-Cornwall-Trebarwith-Strand-Cornwall-02.jpg)
