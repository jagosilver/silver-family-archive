*← [Silver Family Archive](./)*

These are the letters [David Silver](David-Silver) wrote home from boarding school at **[King's School, Bruton](Kings-School-Bruton)**, in Somerset, between **September 1937**, when he arrived aged thirteen, and **June 1942**, when he was eighteen and waiting to join the RAF. His elder brother [Christopher ("Kit")](Christopher-Silver/) had been at the school before him; Christopher's own letter home of May 1936 is on [his page](Christopher-Silver/#bruton-1936). Nearly all of David's letters are addressed to "Daddy and Mummy", the Exeter dentist [Clifford Marking Silver](Clifford-Marking-Silver) and his wife Gladys (née Acott, see [The Acott Letters](Acott-Letters)), at **Tresillian**, the family home in West Avenue, Exeter.

They begin with a homesick new boy counting the days to the next "Sunday leave", drawing his locker and asking for toothpaste, cornflakes and an episcope for Christmas. They run straight through the coming of the war: school debates after Munich, the first wartime term with gas masks, blackout and evacuees, the sinking of the *Royal Oak*, the fall of France, German bombers passing over towards Bristol on the night of the Coventry raid. By the end David is head of his house and of the school's Air Training Corps, sitting an RAF aircrew selection board in Oxford, and, five days after the **Exeter Blitz** of 3–4 May 1942 wrecked Tresillian, writing to his parents that "your extraordinary good fortune in being unhurt more than counterbalances the misfortune of losing the houses." In the same letter he tells them he is to be "the second person in a two seater night-fighter", working the radar.

Throughout he is the man his grandchildren knew: always drawing, mad about photography, building things in his head, reading everything, and very funny.

**About the transcription.** There are sixty-nine letters, the last an unsigned fragment that may be David's or Christopher's. They are transcribed in full with David's own spelling and punctuation. ~~Struck text~~ shows his crossings-out, [?] a doubtful reading, and [square brackets] describe drawings and layout. Page breaks appear as *(p. n)*. Most letters are undated, so each has been dated from postmarks, his own headings or the events he mentions; the evidence is given in italics under each heading, and "c." marks an estimate. The scans for each letter are in the collapsible **Scans** box beneath it.

## 1937

### 01. A Sunday, September 1937 (his first days at Bruton)

*Undated. New boy ("only done an hours work so far and I've only seen 4 masters"), first Sunday leave still to come — his first weeks at King's School, autumn term 1937.*

> *(p. 1)*
> Kings School
> Sunday Bruton
> Dear Mummy
> I hope you are very well, I am getting on quite well, most of the larger boys are very helpful. Yesterday (Saturday) We did what we liked in the morning, and played "Rugger" in the afternoon.
> There are 3 ~~four~~ new boys in our dormitory a prefect and a boy who was here last term a Portugoose (is that right?).
> I shall right to Daddy next week Can you send my sponge at once. I enclose the time table next week as it is in a
>
> *(p. 2)*
> a place where we are not supposed to go. Sunday leave is on the Sunday after next, (NOT The Sunday after today)
> We have only done an hours work (prep) so far and I've only seen 4 masters. I've have found no time when to eat Kellogs Korn Flakes as no one at our table eats them at tea, Please ask Kit about it.
> Dont forget Hairbrush Sponge knife. You may receive a postcard with this which was posted yesterday
> Give my love to the family its only 2 weeks to 3rd Sunday
> with love
> your loving
> Son
> David

### 04. Sunday 7 November 1937

*Dated "November 7th"; Armistice service, fireworks, and the episcope "advertised in Meccano Mag November 1937".*

> *(p. 1)*
> Kings School
> November 7th Bruton
> Dear Mummy and Daddy
> I hope you are very well,
> Contrary to my expectations we had fireworks, and just before November 5th nanny sent me a bob postal order for sweets fireworks
> I promptly spent it on fireworks, Then 4 other boys joined me with a few other fireworks, two boys I like, two boys I don't.
> It was a rotten display owing chiefly to the darkness of the Park The boys I don't like and bad fireworks.
> Those fireworks which did go off
>
> *(p. 2)*
> only fizzed and gave a pop we had two tuppenny rockets one we stuck very gently into the ground and when we light it it stayed there and one that was a dud (Brocks fireworks too).

>

> It was awful and the buglers began bugling (our buglers) and the dog howled like a wolf.
> Its only 3 weeks to 3rd Sunday. Its half-term, I saw the old head master while I was out for a run.
> There are some lovely model houses built by some boys last term they look like this
> [Drawings: two model houses — a long low weatherboarded house with a pitched roof and windows, and a narrow two-storey house.]
>
> *(p. 4)*
> I didn't get much pleasure out of the fireworks because I wasn't doing them at home with a beautifully chalked programme and catherine wheels on walking sticks and things, in fact the thing fell rather flat.
> my watch has gone wrong for the last day or so.

>
> *(p. 5)*

> Hows Kit I haven't heard from him yet.
> To-night 1. Scientific Soc: meet
> 2. MUSICAL CONCERT BY MUSICAL SOC:
>
> *(p. 6)*
> I intend to do a lot of writing to people shortly but I must hurry up with this letter or I shall miss the Post.
> I've decided what I'd rather like FOR XMAS but I better consult you, Daddy first, its easier to get than the Photo electric cell I was telling you about Mummy, last Sunday its the Mills EPiscope (minor I think its called and its 12/6 My Photographs fit it do you think it would be any good, Daddy, it gives 24" by 36" pictures and any kind of the right size picture can be put in it
>
> *(p. 7)*
> it looks like this and its advertised in Meccano Mag November 1937 Which I'd like you please Mummy to get instead of the B.O.P
> [Drawing: the episcope — a box with a projecting lens throwing out rays of light.]
> I don't want any other Meccano Magazines though except Nov + DEC
> I shall want some corn flakes next week I should think near the end and Please send Toothpaste immediately (anything but Philips Dental Magnesia)
> with Love
> your loving
> Son
> David
> [In the bottom corner, boxed off:] I'm afraid this is a scrappy letter
>
> *(p. 8)*

> I have taken all but my last History of Art paper and my last Divinity paper. I am sorry I haven't got that credit in Maths. In the last paper (you have to get nos 1 2 3 right to get a credit) I couldn't get nos 2 or 3 right though I did the rest.
> Please don't expect great things of the Art papers as I haven't really been taught much drawing and the History of Art paper is essential and the set book only arrived today for it having been ordered and ordered for ages.
> I am now head of the house since Pat has gone so I am going to keep an account of the study fund. Since I wrote we have decided that we can just pay the bills on 7/6 each and not ten shillings because an old boy put some money into it on Sunday.
> I am acting in a sketch with another boy at the end of term. Please therefore could you send me a bright blue aertex shirt by way of dress not a pretty pale blue, [continues]
>
> *(p. 2)*
> nice, a yellow one.
> For memory drawing they put a carpenters tool bag before us for ten minutes and then took it away and we had to draw. For Plant drawing we had to draw red berries on a creeper. For imaginative we had to draw "REFUGEES"
> I drew a ~~[struck]~~ wide flat road packed with cars carts cyclists pedestrians leading towards you away from a smoking city over which circled bombers. It required a lot of imagination to guess what it was by the time I had finished.
> I enclose a sheet of paper which apparently enables us to have extra milk (3½ pints) which ought to be useful. You take it to Mrs Hammett and if I know her she gets rather annoyed and doesn't know whatever its for.
> Well its only 9 more days thank Heavens so there isn't much more of this ~~[struck]~~ seemingly never ending term. Don't you think perhaps I would join up after Christmas? Well anyway I may as well finish off now. I assure you I haven't got those extra credits. I can do Science next term and I'd like to, quite. Japan's a blinking nuisance isn't it because its too far away for us to wipe them out, its a pity but still, they could do with it.
> How long do you say the war is going to last I should say about two more years. Well Goodbye till I write again and perhaps that will be the last time this term
> with love your loving son David

*Notes:* Japan entered the war 7–8 Dec 1941; Pirie's boat "leaves for India in December" (scan 192). So probably Tuesday 9 December 1941 (or 2 Dec). "9 more days" to end of term + Japan now at war → Tuesday 9 or 16 December 1941.

### 64. A Sunday, c. March 1942

*Undated. Volunteering for R.A.F. aircrew or rescue launches; expects call-up in June.*

> *(p. 1)*
> King's School
> Bruton.
> Sunday.
> Dear Daddy and Mummy
> Look here I do hope you didn't give your name at the R.A.F Office as I wasn't going to tell them about Oxford as Donald said it would be better not.
> I volunteered by post to Exeter on Thursday.
> Donald had an air ministry pamphlet which said observers may be colour blind and need only have 6/36 sight. Yesterday an erratum arrived for the pamphlet saying that the paragraph about colour blindness was wrong but colour blind cadets in the A.T.C. who had aircrew training might be excepted. Well I have A/C training.
>
> *(p. 2)*
> So I am going to get Donald to lend me the pamphlet and write me a letter to take with me.
> Anyway as regards R.A.F rescue launches. Colour blindness and bad sight are not likely to matter. I can see quite reasonably well without my glasses and my swimming would be all right I think.
> The one fly in the ointment is that an Royal Navy Commander yesterday said he thought all their officers and many of their men were ex merchant navy!
> He suggested R.N. motor torpedo boats. But the snag is that I feel that they wouldn't have a colourblind person in the Navy: They don't mind about glasses though.
>
> *(p. 3)*
> My intention is first to try this observer business if that fails offer to volunteer for motor boats if I can pass the medical and then while I am reserved go and do a months training before joining.
> I don't think I shall have to register until just after I get home.
> I shan't be called up until June probably. If I go into the army I intend getting a commission but I would rather be an A.C.2. in the R.A.F. than a lieutenant in the army.
> I am getting tired of mucking about. I shall hurry things up if they let me but Donald is actually keen on my not doing anything until the holidays.
> The headmaster will let me do anything though thank heavens!
> P.S. hows Wiggs?
> With love
> your loving son.
> David.
>
> *(p. 4)*

*Notes:* registration for service + call-up "until June" + A.T.C. suggests early 1942 (David turned 18 on 2 Jan 1942) — i.e. spring term 1942. Later than the 1936–40 range.

### 65. Saturday 9 May 1942

*Dated "Saturday 9th" on printed school paper, written after the Exeter Blitz (3–4 May 1942). Page order: 216, 218, 217, 219 (scans 220–223 are rescans).*

> *(p. 1)*
> KING'S SCHOOL,
> BRUTON,
> SOMERSET.
> Saturday 9th
> My Dear Daddy & Mummy
> Thank you very much for your letter which was very welcome.
> It is quite useless for me to try to say how glad I am you are safe, or how sorry I am that you had such a ghastly time, because I can't express my feelings on paper.
> I have gleaned a certain amount of information about Exeter from various sources in regard to the state of the damage done.
> I feel that every one was and is, equally exposed to the same amount of risk and that it was just bad luck that we suffered. I also feel that your extraordinary good fortune in being unhurt more than counterbalances
>
> *(p. 2)*
> the misfortune of losing the houses.
> I now find that I am allowed to tell you a little more fully the nature of my future work. I shall be the second person in a two seater night-fighter.
> I shall have radio apparatus by which I can locate enemy aircraft.
> I may have four synchronized machine guns or cannon to fire
> I shall be used in the defence of places against bombing attacks.
> I believe that Lavender's work is the same only on the ground and without the guns.
> Please don't tell people this.
> It is all secret and an entirely new branch of the Royal Air Force and the Huns would very much like to know about it.
> What little I have told you is probably known to them all ready but I would rather you didn't talk about it just the same.
>
> *(p. 3)*
> I should rather like to know your plans at some time. Are you ever going back to Tresillian, I hope not.
> It wouldn't be much fun living in a road like that.
> Also as regards the practice, will you retire Daddy? or what?
> The Germans have new apparatus working in their bombers which we must at all costs get without damaging. I hope we brought down one or two bombers fairly intact.
> I have visions of your retiring to a small house in the country; I hope you can – I suppose it isn't very likely though.
> I wonder if Pinders are still there they had two pairs of my trousers they were altering. I don't quite know how much Tresillian there is from your letter Mummy.
> I don't suppose I can do anything but stay here through Corpus till I get called up, can I?
> I wish that I could come down and
>
> *(p. 4)*
> help you clear up, although at present I feel as if I never want to go to West Avenue again. Please do write sometime when you have a minute and say what you are going to do and if I can help.
> I would like Lavender, Christopher and Philip's new addresses sometime as at present James is the only person I can write to.
> Please excuse this writing paper being so dirty, only I tried to write this during a cricket match in longish grass. I suppose the Leggett, legged it.
> I wish you would come and live up here somewhere. It's so far from anywhere but Wells.
> Well I'll finish off here. Wasn't it silly of me I wrote you a letter last Sunday congratulating you on having no fire watching on Saturday night, I might have guessed something would happen. I don't expect you got it. I am a Flt Sgt now in the A.T.C.
> well I must finish now
> with lots of love
> your loving son David.
>
> *(p. 5)*
>
> *(p. 6)*
>
> *(p. 7)*
>
> *(p. 8)*

*Notes:* Saturday 9 May 1942 (Exeter Blitz, 4 May 1942). he is describing training as a radar operator / navigator (radio) in a two-seat night fighter — airborne interception radar, then secret. the raid was the night of Sunday 3 – Monday 4 May 1942; Tresillian, West Avenue, Exeter was evidently damaged or destroyed ("losing the houses").
