An autobiographical essay written by **Jago Silver** (then Jago Edward Titcomb) aged 15, during Year 10 at Wadebridge School, Summer Term 1995. It describes an evening sailing on the River Exe at [Topsham](Anthea-Silver) — the same town where his grandmother [Anthea Silver](Anthea-Silver) grew up and which she wrote about in *A Topsham Childhood*.

# A Trip on the River

*Candidate Name: Jago Edward Titcomb*
*Candidate Number: 1153*
*Summer Term 1995*

Around five years ago we moved from Topsham, near Exeter in Devon, to
St.Mabyn in Cornwall. Every now and then we returned there to visit
friends and go shopping in the "big city"!

Topsham is on the bank of the estuary of the River Exe, however, though
I had lived there for nearly 11 years I had only ever twice been out on
it in a boat! The first time was on a brief canoeing excursion with my
friend and his dad; the second was in the summer holidays when I went on
a weeks sailing course. This in itself was a memorable experience as I
was taught by someone who assumed that we were all born in a boat, and
most of the time I was terrified of him, especially when he said that
he'd drown the lot of us if we capsized!

So I had only been on the river twice which was a pity as in my opinion
the River Exe is one of the beautiful...On the far side of the river
were enormous forests of reeds with numerous streams and creeks winding
in and out of them in a vein-like labyrinth of waterways. On windy days
the reeds rippled and swayed as if they too were part of the river.

As I was saying, every now and then we would return to visit friends, I
particularly enjoyed visiting our old landlord and landlady; Marlene and
Richard and their 25-year-old son Matthew. On one occasion I stayed with
them for the weekend while the rest of my family stayed with friends in
Exeter. When I arrived it turned out that Matt and Richard were sailing
in a race on the Saturday and Matt suggested that I come along and see
if anyone needed someone to crew for them. I was, to say the least, a
little apprehensive, the last time I had been in a boat was around 5
years ago in a terrified trance-like state. Also I had been sailing
"Toppers" which were miniature, 1 sailed, 8 ft boats, the boats in the
race were 17 ft "Hornets" with two more sails! I took some spare
clothes and went with Matt and Richard to the sailing club.

The sailing club was full of very "boaty" people and Matt found me an
interesting guy called Mike. He was wearing wellies tucked into which
were a battered pair of jeans, a Guernsey, and a white captain's cap.
He was in short the archetypal sailor, he seemed quite willing to take
me on so I went and changed. As I changed I was thinking, "What on
earth am I doing? I'm going sailing in a race and I hardly know how to
sail!"

Mike and I set out before the others so that I could get a bit of
practice; we sailed in circles for a while and then lined up. When the
starting whistle blew we moved off slowly down river, at first I was
busy swinging the jib sail from one side to the other as we went about,
but after a while the wind settled down and I was able to look around. I
had never seen Topsham from the river before as the previous trips had
been either further up or down river. We sailed on for around an hour
gradually getting slower and slower. We kept going but before we got to
the marker buoy, that was the turning point, the wind dropped
completely. We let the sails right out to try and catch any wind, and
suddenly it came, unfortunately it came from the wrong direction! We set
the sails and turned back, having given up the race.

Before long the wind dropped again, by now it was around 8.30 (we had
set out at 6.30), the sun was low in the sky and Mike suggested that I
paddle for a bit while he steered. This I did for about 15 minutes and
then he said he'd let me steer, whilst steering I had a chance to look
around. The low sun and the absence of wind made a very calm and serene
scene. The only noise was the distant rumble of traffic, the murmur of
voices coming from other river users, and the ever present whispering
and rustling of the reeds. After another turn at paddling I was steering
again, Mike suggested that I steered close to the bank as occasionally a
little wind was to be found there. As we drew nearer to the bank I heard
a terrific crashing and beating sound coming from the reeds, I looked
towards the sound just in time to see two enormous herons, they flapped,
arduously into the air and when enough height had been gained, slipped
effortlessly up river.

We half drifted, carried by the tide, and half paddled our way up river,
once or twice I saw fish jumping, Mike said they were salmon and that
before now he had caught them. As we neared the sailing club I was
steering, it was nearly dark and the club lights looked very inviting,
however it wasn't possible to concentrate on that as it was getting
more difficult to navigate between the numerous yachts and dinghies
moored in the river. I glanced up river and saw, in the distance the
motor way bridge, it looked like a many eyed monster floating across the
river, as all I could see was the headlights of the cars. We sculled
slowly towards the sailing club, the bright white floodlights and groups
of people gave a busy look to the place and as the keel of the boat
grated against the slip way I felt sorry to be leaving the peace and
tranquillity of the river and stepping back into the real world.
